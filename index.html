Where physics meets data

AIMS is built on computational condensed matter physics: density functional theory (DFT) calculations of real materials, checked against X-ray absorption, infrared, photoemission and other experiments. Machine learning sits on top of that foundation. It makes the physics faster and reaches problems that physics alone cannot.

DFT and first principles

Electronic structure, band gap engineering and defect formation energies with VASP and Quantum ESPRESSO.

Spectroscopy simulation

XAS, XPS and IR spectra that fingerprint local structure and validate experimental data.

Applied AI and ML

Materials discovery, phase identification and image analysis for medical and agricultural problems.

Condensed matter physics and DFT

The foundation of the group. First-principles calculations of defects, interfaces, surfaces and layered materials, always tied back to a measurement.

01

Defects in oxides

Where is the hydrogen, manganese or calcium atom, and what is it doing? We compute vibrational modes and X-ray absorption spectra for candidate defect sites and match them to infrared and XANES measurements in SrTiO3, BaTiO3 and CdTe.

Interfaces and surfaces

Strain, intermixing and the two-dimensional electron gas at LaAlO3/SrTiO3, and what core-level binding energy shifts in XPS reveal about the species sitting on a surface.

2D materials and photoemission

Band structures of HfSe2 and other layered materials, tuned by strain and alkali-metal dosing, compared directly with angle-resolved photoemission. MXenes and negative electronic compressibility.

AI for materials and data

Machine learning built on top of that physics: faster spectra, smarter screening, and real-world data problems from medicine to energy markets.

01

Spectra from structure, and back

Neural networks that predict XANES spectra from atomic structure instantly, inverse models that recover structure from XAS with calibrated uncertainty, and automated phase identification from XRD patterns.

XANESXAS inversionXRDUncertainty
02

ML-guided materials screening

Predicting the effect of cationic dopants on hydroxyapatite from large databases, then checking the best candidates against DFT elastic tensors. Neural networks for the mechanical properties of bio-cement.

03

Deep learning for imaging

Diabetic retinopathy classification from fundus images, optical coherence tomography with deep learning for chick gender determination, and real-time pupil tracking for retinal imaging.

Forecasting and data science

Walk-forward benchmarks of gradient boosting and deep models for electricity prices on the Japan Electric Power Exchange, and neural networks for black hole mass in X-ray reverberating AGNs.

Learn to compute at scale

Our DFT and machine-learning work runs on high-performance computing (HPC) clusters. Students learn to use them well, a skill that carries into academic research and into industry careers in computational science and data science.

  1. Work on a Linux cluster. Shell, file systems, modules, remote editing and sharing data safely.
  2. Submit and manage batch jobs. Write job scripts, use the scheduler queue, monitor runs and recover failed ones.
  3. Run DFT in parallel. VASP and Quantum ESPRESSO across many cores, with convergence tests and scaling checks before a big run.
  4. Automate workflows. Python scripts that build structures, launch many calculations and parse the results.
  5. Turn results into models. Collect the calculated data and train machine-learning models on it.
